Brats in Red Wine, Dressed Up

Other135 min totalserves 4cozy weather

Bratwurst braised in red wine over a silky apple and celery root puree. Fancy enough for guests, easy enough that you'll still pretend it was hard.

Before we start

The puree is what makes this dish. Tart apple keeps the earthy celery root from going heavy, and a blender makes it silky. Be patient with the blending. Nobody wants gritty puree under a fancy sausage.

Ingredients

For the puree

  • 4 cupwater
  • 2 cuppeeled chopped celery root
  • 1small Granny Smith applepeeled, cored, and chopped, about 1 cup
  • 3 tbspunsalted butter
  • ¾ tspkosher salt
  • ⅛ tspblack pepper

For the bratwurst

  • 2 tbspvegetable oil
  • 1 lbbratwurst linksabout 4 links
  • 10 ozking oyster mushroomscut lengthwise into 1/2-inch-thick planks (about 1 1/2 inches long), about 2 cups
  • 6 ozcipollini onions (6 to 8 onions)peeled and halved
  • 1 cup(8 oz.) dry red wine
  • 1 cupbeef stock
  • 3 tbspunsalted butter
  • 2 tbspsliced scallions
  • 1 tbspfresh lemon juice
  • ½ tspkosher salt
  • ⅛ tspblack pepper
  • Dill frondsfor garnish

Instructions

  1. Make the puree. Put the 4 cups water, celery root and apple in a medium saucepan and bring to a boil over high heat. Turn the heat down to medium and cook until tender, 8 to 10 minutes. Reserve 1/4 cup of the cooking liquid.
  2. Drain the celery root mixture through a fine wire-mesh strainer. Tip it into a blender with the butter, salt and pepper. Process until smooth, about 2 minutes. Add the reserved cooking liquid 1 tablespoon at a time, only as needed, until the puree is silky and smooth. Pour it back into the saucepan, cover and keep warm.
  3. Make the bratwurst. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the bratwurst and cook, turning occasionally, until browned on all sides, 6 to 8 minutes. Move them to a plate.
  4. Add the mushrooms to the same skillet. Cook, turning occasionally, until golden, 3 to 4 minutes. Move them to the plate. Add the onions and cook, turning occasionally, until lightly browned, 2 to 3 minutes. Move them to the plate with the mushrooms. Don't wash the skillet. Those brown bits are the sauce.
  5. Pour the wine and stock into the skillet and bring to a boil over high heat, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom. Put the bratwurst back in. Turn the heat down to medium-low, cover, and simmer until heated through, about 10 minutes.
  6. Move the bratwurst to a plate and let them rest for 5 minutes. Turn the heat up to medium-high and cook the wine mixture until the liquid is reduced to about 1 cup, 4 to 5 minutes. It will bubble hard. That's the point.
  7. Return the mushrooms and onions to the skillet. Turn the heat down to medium-low, stir in the butter, and cook, stirring constantly, until the butter is emulsified, about 1 minute. Stir in the scallions, lemon juice, salt and pepper.
  8. Divide the puree among 4 plates and top each with 1 bratwurst. Spoon the mushrooms, onions and sauce over and around the puree. Garnish with dill fronds and serve.

Kitchen's closed Leftovers keep up to 2 days. Refrigerate the puree and the cooked bratwurst and mushrooms in separate airtight containers, then reheat each separately over medium-low.
