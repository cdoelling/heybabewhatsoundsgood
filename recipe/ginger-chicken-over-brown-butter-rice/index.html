Ginger Chicken Over Brown Butter Rice

Asian35 min totalserves 4

Saucy ginger chicken with snap peas and bok choy over nutty brown butter rice. The rice is the real star, and it knows it.

Before we start

The brown butter rice is why this dish is worth making. It takes a few extra minutes, and it turns a forgettable pot of grains into something nutty and toasty. If you need the store-bought shortcut tonight, take it. I won't tell anyone. You can also skip the rice and just eat the stir-fry.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbspunsalted butter
  • 6garlic cloves
  • 1(2-inch) piece fresh ginger
  • 10to 16 ounces snap peashowever big the bag is at your store
  • 1small (not baby) head bok choyabout 1 pound
  • 1½ cups short-grain white ricerinsed
  • ¼ cupplus 2 tablespoons low-sodium soy sauce
  • 1½ pounds bonelessskinless chicken breasts or thighs
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 tbspplus 2 teaspoons all-purpose flour
  • Neutral oil
  • ¼ cupfresh orange juice
  • 2 tbsphoney or brown sugarlight or dark
  • Toppings (optional): sesame seeds (toastedif you have them!), sriracha

Instructions

  1. Melt the butter in a small pot over medium-low heat until it foams. Then cook until the milk solids drop to the bottom and turn brown and the butterfat turns golden brown, 5 to 6 minutes total.
  2. While that happens, mince the garlic. Mince or grate the ginger, no need to peel it. Cut the snap peas into 1-inch pieces if you want, or leave them whole. Roughly chop the bok choy.
  3. Add the rice and half the garlic to the pot with the brown butter. Stir to coat and cook until toasted, about 1 minute. Stir in 2½ cups water and 2 tablespoons of the soy sauce. Turn the heat up to high and bring to a boil. Then turn it down to low, cover, and cook for 15 minutes. Take it off the heat and let it stand, covered, for 10 minutes, or until ready to serve. Resist peeking.
  4. Meanwhile, trim any fat off the chicken and cut the meat into 1-inch cubes. Season with ½ teaspoon salt and some pepper. Sprinkle with 2 tablespoons of the flour and toss to coat. It's okay if it doesn't all stick, but you want every piece covered in flour. Your thumb is a fine ruler.
  5. Warm enough oil to coat the bottom of your largest skillet (about 2 tablespoons for a 12-inch skillet) over medium-high heat. While it heats, stir together the remaining ¼ cup soy sauce, the orange juice, honey, remaining garlic, the ginger and the remaining 2 teaspoons flour in a small bowl.
  6. When the oil is shimmering, add the chicken in an even layer. Cook it undisturbed until browned all over, 4 to 6 minutes per side, flipping halfway through. Add the snap peas and bok choy, scattering them around and on top of the chicken. Cook until bright green, another minute. Pour in the sauce and cook, stirring, until it thickens, 1 to 3 minutes. Keep a close eye on it: you want saucy, not reduced to the point of burning.
  7. Uncover the rice and fluff it gently with a fork. Build bowls with rice, chicken and veg, and plenty of sauce. Finish with sesame seeds and sriracha, if you like. Enjoy!

Kitchen's closed Short on time next round? Stir butter, garlic powder and salt into store-bought frozen rice once it's cooked, and call it a day.
