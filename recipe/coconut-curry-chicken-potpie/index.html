Potpie, Curried Away

Asian70 min totalserves 6cozy weather

Coconut curry chicken and sweet potato under a golden puff pastry lid, all in one skillet. A hug with a crust.

Before we start

The filling gets its body from blended sweet potato and coconut milk instead of a flour roux. It's silky, it's sneaky, and you get to feel like a scientist for about ninety seconds. A 12-inch cast-iron skillet is the right tool here.

Ingredients

  • 1large yellow onionchopped, about 1½ cups
  • ¼ cupShaoxing wineor dry sherry
  • 1 lbrusset potatoespeeled and cut into ½-inch cubes, about 3 cups
  • 1 cupthawed frozen peas
  • 2 tbspoyster sauce
  • 5 tbspneutral cooking oil (such as canola)divided
  • 1(2-inch) piece fresh gingerpeeled and minced, about 2 Tbsp.
  • 5large garlic clovesminced, about 5 tsp.
  • 1 tbspMadras curry powder
  • 1 lbsweet potatoespeeled and cut into ½-inch cubes, about 3 cups
  • 2 cuplower-sodium chicken broth
  • 2 tspgranulated sugar
  • 2 tspkosher salt
  • 1 tspblack pepper
  • 1 tspMSG (such as Ajinomoto)optional
  • ½(17.3-oz.) pkg. frozen puff pastrythawed
  • All-purpose flourfor dusting
  • 1large eggbeaten
  • 1 lbbonelessskinless chicken thighs, cut into ½-inch pieces, about 2 cups
  • 1(13.5-oz.) can unsweetened coconut milkpreferably Chaokoh
  • 2 tbspsoy sauce

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F with racks in the upper and lower thirds. Line a baking sheet with parchment. On a lightly floured counter, roll the puff pastry into a 12-inch square, then trim it into a 12-inch round and discard the scraps. Cut 3 small slits in the center. Set it on the baking sheet, brush it evenly with egg and chill until you need it. Cold pastry puffs. Warm pastry slumps. Keep it in the fridge.
  2. Toss the chicken with the oyster sauce in a medium bowl and set aside. Heat 2 tablespoons oil in a 12-inch cast-iron skillet over medium. Add the ginger and garlic and cook, stirring often, until fragrant and soft, about 2 minutes. Add the curry powder and cook, stirring constantly, until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  3. Stir in the sweet potatoes, broth, sugar, salt, pepper and MSG (if using). Bring to a simmer over medium, then lower to medium-low and cook, stirring now and then, until the sweet potatoes are tender, about 10 minutes. Take it off the heat.
  4. Transfer to a blender and add the coconut milk and soy sauce. Secure the lid, remove the center piece so steam can escape, and cover the opening with a clean towel. Blend until smooth, about 1 minute. Set the purée aside and wipe the skillet clean. Hot liquid in a sealed blender will blow the lid off. Ask me how I know.
  5. Heat the remaining 3 tablespoons oil in the skillet over medium-high. Add the chicken and cook, undisturbed, until golden underneath, about 4 minutes. Flip, add the onion and cook, stirring now and then, until soft, 4 to 5 minutes. Add the Shaoxing wine and scrape up the browned bits.
  6. Stir in the sweet potato purée, russet potatoes and peas and bring to a simmer over medium-high. Take it off the heat.
  7. Move the skillet to the lower rack and put the baking sheet with the pastry round on the upper rack. Bake until the pastry is deeply golden, the filling bubbles at the edges and the potatoes are tender, 25 to 30 minutes. Pull both out and let them stand 10 minutes, then set the pastry on top of the potpie and serve right away. Ten minutes is a long time to stare at a potpie. Do it anyway. The filling is lava.

Kitchen's closed Deeply golden means deeply golden. Pale puff pastry is just a damp cracker with ambitions.
