Hot Italian Sandwich, Napkins Required

American30 min totalserves 4

A chopped pork sausage-style sandwich with giardiniera aioli, melty provolone and a toasted hoagie roll. It's loud, it's messy, and you'll eat it over the sink anyway.

Before we start

The pork wants to sit still and brown before you touch it. Resist the urge to poke. Browning is where the flavor lives, and you'll chop everything together afterward anyway.

Ingredients

Giardiniera-Garlic Aioli

  • 4garlic cloves
  • ½ tspkosher salt
  • ½ cupmayonnaise
  • 2 tbspjarred giardiniera brinesee Note
  • 1 tspfresh lemon juice

Sandwich

  • 1½ lbground pork
  • 2 tspfennel seedslightly crushed
  • 1 tspkosher salt
  • ½ tspblack pepper
  • 1 tbspneutral cooking oilsuch as canola
  • ½ cupdrained jarred giardinierachopped
  • 2 tbspdrained jarred sliced sport pepperssee Note
  • 1 tbspjarred chopped Calabrian chiles
  • 6 sliceprovolone or mozzarella cheese
  • 4(about 7-inch) hoagie rolls or po'boy rollssplit lengthwise and lightly toasted

Instructions

  1. Make the giardiniera-garlic aioli: finely chop the garlic and sprinkle it with the salt. Smash it with the flat side of a knife until it forms a smooth paste. Stir the garlic paste, mayonnaise, giardiniera brine and lemon juice together in a small bowl until combined. Set the aioli aside.
  2. Make the sandwich: stir the pork, fennel seeds, salt and black pepper together in a large bowl until just combined. Don't overwork it. Form the mixture into 4 oblong patties, 6 inches long and 3 inches wide.
  3. Heat the oil in a large cast-iron skillet over medium-high. Cook the patties, undisturbed, until well browned on the bottom side, 5 to 6 minutes. It will sizzle loudly. That's the good sound.
  4. Flip the patties and chop them into small pieces with a metal spatula. Add the giardiniera, sport peppers and Calabrian chiles. Cook, chopping everything together, until the meat is cooked through and finely crumbled, about 3 minutes.
  5. Spread the meat mixture into an even layer and top with the cheese. Let it stand for 1 minute. Turn off the heat and chop to combine. Yes, more chopping. Your spatula is earning its keep.
  6. Spread the reserved aioli evenly on the cut sides of the rolls. Using a slotted spoon, transfer the pork mixture, evenly divided, to the bottom halves of the rolls (about 1 cup each). Top with the top halves of the rolls. Serve hot.

Kitchen's closed Leftover aioli keeps in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days, so make a second sandwich tomorrow and pretend it was planned.
