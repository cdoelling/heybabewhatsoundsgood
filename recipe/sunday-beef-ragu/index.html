Sunday Ragù, No Clock

Italian210 min totalserves 6cozy weather

Beef braised low and slow in wine and tomatoes until it falls apart, tossed with pappardelle. Worth every one of its three hours.

Before we start

This is a Sunday recipe because it needs three hours in the oven, and Sunday is the only day you're home long enough to smell it the whole time. Brown the beef properly at the start. That dark crust is where the ragù gets its depth.

Ingredients

  • 3 lbbeef chuck roastcut into large chunks
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 tbspolive oil
  • 1yellow oniondiced
  • 2carrotsdiced
  • 2celery stalksdiced
  • 4garlic clovesminced
  • 2 tbsptomato paste
  • 1 cupdry red wine
  • 28 ozcanned whole tomatoescrushed by hand
  • 2 cupbeef broth
  • 1 lbpappardelle
  • 2 ozparmesangrated

Instructions

  1. Season the beef generously and brown it hard in the olive oil in a Dutch oven, in batches. Set it aside. In batches. Crowd the pot and it steams instead of browning, and that's a lot of flavor left on the table.
  2. Lower the heat and cook the onion, carrot and celery until soft and sweet, about 10 minutes.
  3. Add the garlic and tomato paste and cook until the paste darkens, about 2 minutes.
  4. Pour in the wine and scrape the bottom of the pot clean. Reduce by half.
  5. Add the tomatoes, broth and the beef with its juices. Bring to a bare simmer.
  6. Cover and braise in a 325°F oven for 3 hours, until the beef collapses under a spoon. Then go live your life. Come back occasionally to sniff and feel smug.
  7. Shred the beef into the sauce, skim the fat and season again.
  8. Boil the pappardelle, then toss it through the ragù with a splash of pasta water and the Parmesan.

Kitchen's closed Freeze half the ragù. Your future self will find it on a terrible Tuesday and feel very loved.
